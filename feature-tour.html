Lumen Reader feature tour

One section per feature. Each says what to try (keys, or words to say while holding Ctrl+Opt) and what to expect. Lumen Reader's commands use Ctrl+Opt held together. Everything here is on this page; nothing is sent anywhere.

“The cursor” below means the Lumen Reader cursor: the outline around the item being read. It follows keyboard focus (where typing goes), and Ctrl+Opt with the arrow keys moves it on its own, like the VoiceOver cursor. It isn't the mouse pointer or the text insertion point.

Section 18 (audit) has accessibility mistakes on purpose, and section 10 has two buttons and a field left unnamed on purpose. Everything else is meant to be correct.

1. Moving and reading

Ctrl+Opt+Right and Ctrl+Opt+Left through this section; Ctrl+Opt+Home / Ctrl+Opt+End; Ctrl+Opt+A to read all (any key stops it); Ctrl+Opt+Z repeats; tap Control to pause.

Each item spoken as name, status, type ("Save, button"); the outline follows and the page scrolls to it; a low tone at the start or end; read all uses the long-reading voice.

This paragraph is ordinary text to read. The next one is too.

Turn Lumen Reader off and on with Ctrl+Opt+Cmd+S; lock Ctrl+Opt with Ctrl+Opt+;.

2. Find and the rotor

Ctrl+Opt+Cmd+H (next heading), L (link), J (form control), T (table), X (list), G (image); add Shift to go back. Ctrl+Opt+U opens the rotor: Left/Right for links, headings, form controls; type to filter; Return to go.

"heading level 3, Find practice"; "No next table" with a falling tone when there's none.

7. Reading mode

Ctrl+Opt+Shift+R anywhere on this page (or say "reading mode").

Reading starts at section 1 (the main content), skips the contents list (navigation), the sidebar and the footer, reads links and text as prose, and says "Image: …" and "Video: …"; any key stops it.

A short article

Reading mode is for long text. It leaves out the parts of a page that are not the content: menus, banners, sidebars and footers, and it doesn't stop on every button.

It reads links inside a sentence as part of the sentence, the way a person would.

8. Ask about the screen

Ctrl+Opt+Shift+S to summarize the page. Ctrl+Opt+/ and type (or hold Ctrl+Opt and say): "Where is the Save progress button?", "Is there a video?", "How many tables are there?", "Which fields are required?" Then a follow-up: "What is the second one about?" Up in the box recalls earlier questions.

A tone, then the answer; "Thinking" only if it takes over 3 seconds. "Where is…" moves the cursor there (then Ctrl+Opt+Space presses it); "Is there a video?" says how many and lists them to choose from.

9. Videos, images and pictures

Ask "Is there a video?" or "Are there images?". Put the cursor on the chart or the sign and press Ctrl+Opt+Shift+L to describe it (needs Screen Recording permission).

"2 videos" listed; the chart described ("a bar chart … sales rising from January to April"); the sign's words read out.

11. Dictation

Focus a field, hold Ctrl+Opt (half a second), speak, release. In the email field say "jane at example dot com", or spell it: "j a n e at example dot com". In the notes, say two sentences.

A rising tone, your words in the captions, a falling tone, the text inserted and read once. The email comes out as an address (jane@example.com); the one-line field has no final full stop; the notes keep theirs. Outside a text field (and the ask box), holding Ctrl+Opt does nothing, however long.

12. Voice commands

Open the ask box (Ctrl+Opt+/), then hold Ctrl+Opt and say (or type):

  • "Change speech rate to 60", "Speak faster", "Volume 80", "Verbosity low"
  • "Make the captions high contrast yellow and blue", "Make the captions bigger", "Reset caption colors"
  • "Turn off hints", "Hide the captions", "Turn the cursor on"
  • "Go to the nickname field", "Press Save progress", "Press Delete account" (risky: only moves there)
  • "Reading mode", "Describe this table", "Check before sending", "Audit this page", "Fill this form", "Open settings"

Settings change and are confirmed ("Rate 60"; "Captions: yellow on blue, contrast 9.5 to 1"); "Press Save progress" presses it and you hear "Saved 1 time"; "Delete account" is not pressed.

13. Fill this form

First add your details in Settings › Your details. Put the cursor in this form (for example on First name), then Ctrl+Opt+Shift+F (or say "fill this form"). While the plan is shown, hold Ctrl+Opt and say "delivery notes, leave it at the door". Return fills, Escape cancels.

"I can fill 8 fields: First name, …; … Not filled: Delivery notes, Promo code." After the dictation, delivery notes added. After Return, each field read back and "Not submitted". The password and promo code stay empty.

14. Check before sending

Ctrl+Opt+Shift+C (or say "check before sending").

Put the cursor in this form first (for example on the To field), so the check is about it.

"3 things to check": To is required and empty; a typo ("recieve", maybe "receive"); the message says something is attached, but nothing is. Each in a list to jump to. Fill the To field, fix the typo and remove "attached" to hear "Looks ready to send."

16. Apps, windows and settings

Hold Cmd and press Tab a few times; release to switch. Open a new window. Choose Settings… in the menu-bar menu (Cmd+,) and change something. Drag the caption panel anywhere, including another display; press its ✕ to quit (it says "Closing Lumen Reader").

Each app named as you Tab ("Slack, 2 of 11"), then the app, window and focused item; the settings window read like any app, its changes applied at once.

17. The companion window

Press Ctrl+Opt+Shift+G once, then twice (or choose Lumen Reader in the Dock). In the window, Cmd+1 to Cmd+7 switch sections (Cmd+7, Keys, lists every key; type to find one) and Cmd+W closes it. In Ask, type "Which windows are open?". In Make it mine, type "speak slower in Mail" and press Return. Back on this page, press Start download below, put the cursor on the progress bar and press Ctrl+Opt+Shift+W.

Once: a spoken glance ("You're in Safari, Lumen Reader feature tour. 9 windows open."). Twice: the window opens and lands on the section's summary. Ask answers with Go to buttons. Make it mine reads the rule back ("In Mail: speak slower (rate −15).") and waits for Use this rule. The watch is listed in the Watch section with its progress, and you're told once when it finishes, about 30 seconds later.

18. Accessibility audit (mistakes on purpose)

Ctrl+Opt+Shift+A (or say "audit this page").

A spoken summary, an Issues list to jump to, and a report in Documents › Lumen Reader Audits. It should find everything planted below (other sections may add a few, such as the unlabeled buttons in section 10):

  • A heading that skips a level (level 4 after level 2) — 1.3.1
  • An image with no description — 1.1.1
  • A button with no name and a field with no label — 4.1.2, 1.3.1
  • Links that say only "click here" and "read more" — 2.4.4
  • Two links both called "Report" going to different places — 2.4.4
  • Tiny buttons packed together — 2.5.8
